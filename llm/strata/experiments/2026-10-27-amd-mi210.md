# 2026-10-27 — AMD MI210: Qwen3.8-Flash-Next on 2× gfx90a (llama.cpp HIP)

The unmeasured AMD point. **r50** carries 2× **AMD Instinct MI210 64 GB**
(gfx90a, CDNA2), 384 EPYC 9654 CPUs and 1.5 TB RAM under ROCm 6.2. This
campaign builds **llama.cpp b11785 for gfx90a** with the CDNA2 matrix-core and
flash-attention options it actually has, and measures the three pinned GGUF
packs fully resident. It is a **llama.cpp** point, not a Strata point; the
CUDA rows it is compared with were measured with the Strata engine, and that
engine difference is part of the result.

Headline:

| question | answer |
|:--|:--|
| does it beat the CUDA rigs on decode? | **No.** 38.6 tok/s at 4 k and 24.4 at 262 k, vs L40 154, V100-4× 96 / 72.5, Titan-2× ~87 / 68. |
| does it beat them on €/Mtok? | **Only partly.** Decode-only energy is €0.44/Mtok at 16 k and €0.93 at 262 k (V100-4×: €0.65 at 262 k); at 262 k a short answer is prefill-dominated at €31.6/Mtok. A **derived** 2 007-token answer at 262 k (measured prefill + decode + power) is €4.83/Mtok vs V100-4× €5.81, so energy per token is roughly tied while throughput is 1.7× lower. |
| is it HBM-limited? | **No.** The 5.16 GB/token active-bytes roofline is 310 tok/s (paper 1.6 TB/s) or 259 tok/s (measured 1.336 TB/s). Measured η_CDNA is **0.15 at 4 k** and **0.09 at 262 k**; the GPUs sit at 1 700 MHz, ~100–200 W/card, with the CPU 99.6 % idle. It is a kernel/latency limit, not a bandwidth limit. |
| is it fully resident? | **Yes for the transformer**: all 48 layers + KV on GPU, 68.98 GB of 131.04 GB at 262 k. The 28.8 GB PLE table is host-mapped by design (`TENSOR_READ_LAZY`) and gathers 16 rows/token; no expert streaming from SSD. |

## 4. Protocol

- `-ngl 99`, f16 KV, `-fa on`, layer split (`-sm layer`, default), 2 cards.
- **pp/tg** with `llama-bench`: `-p <ctx−1> -n 0` for prompt processing and
  `-p 0 -n 128 -d <ctx−1>` for generation at depth. The `−1` is deliberate:
  b11785 cannot restore the hybrid KV state between repetitions, and one
  `-r 2` run at an exact 4096 depth hit a GPU memory access fault. Every bench
  cell is therefore a single repetition; repeats live in the server cells.
- **TTFT/decode** with a running `llama-server -c 262144 -np 1` and
  `probe_client.py`. TTFT = server `prompt_ms` + one `predicted_per_token_ms`;
  the `prompt_n` reported is the server's, not the target.
- **C2** with `-np 2` (2×131 k slots), two simultaneous requests.
- **q8_0 KV** with `-ctk q8_0 -ctv q8_0` at 64 k/262 k.
- **Power** with `power_sampler.py` reading amdgpu hwmon at 1 Hz: 30 s idle,
  then windows around the 16 k and 262 k cells. Only the GPU package power is
  measured; CPUs, fans and PSU losses are excluded.

The full job (38246877) ran 2 h 24 min. Staging to `/dev/shm` took 11 s + 19 s
for the two IQ3_S shards, 458 s for Coder and 149 s for Q2_0; the 28.8 GB PLE
shard was hardlinked into each pack. All server loads took **10 s** from
`/dev/shm`.

## 6. Residency

All transformer layers and the KV cache are on the two cards; nothing is
streamed from SSD. VRAM at a 262 144-token server:

| pack | GPU0 | GPU1 | total | of 131.04 GB |
|:--|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| IQ3_S f16 | 33.94 GB | 35.03 GB | **68.98 GB** | 53 % |
| Coder f16 | 21.49 GB | 22.23 GB | 43.72 GB | 33 % |
| Q2_0 f16 | 26.37 GB | 25.41 GB | 51.78 GB | 40 % |
| IQ3_S q8_0 KV | 32.63 GB | 33.72 GB | 66.35 GB | 51 % |
| IQ3_S `-np 2` | 33.06 GB | 34.15 GB | 67.21 GB | 51 % |

The remaining IQ3_S model bytes are the **28.8 GB `per_layer_token_embd`
table**, which llama.cpp creates with `TENSOR_READ_LAZY`: it lives in host RAM
and each token gathers 16 rows × 160 values (1.4 KB). It is not expert
streaming. Forcing it onto GPU0 with
`-ot per_layer_token_embd=ROCm0` worked and was **2 % slower** (37.82 vs 38.57
tok/s at 4 k), so the host-mapped path is the right default. The pack is fully
resident in the 128 GB GPU + 1.5 TB RAM envelope; the "fully resident" claim
is about the transformer and KV, not about putting the lazy PLE table in VRAM.

Server load from `/dev/shm` is **10 s** for every pack, including IQ3_S.

## 7. Roofline and η_CDNA

The dataset's 2.54 GB/token figure assumes 6 B active at 3.38 bpw. The actual
GGUF's per-token traffic is larger, because the non-expert trunk (attention,
hyper-connections, shared expert, output) is read every token:

```text
expert bytes            50.29 GB     (10 of 512 experts active -> 0.98 GB/token)
non-expert bytes         4.51 GB     (token_embd 0.34 GB excluded: one row)
PLE gather               1.4 KB      (16 rows x 160 values, IQ4_NL)
active bytes/token       5.159 GB    (11.8 B params-equivalent at 3.49 bpw)
```

Roofline for a single token stream through a 2-card **layer split** is serial:
the two halves do not overlap, so it is `B_total / BW_one_card`, not
`B_total / (2 BW)`.

| bandwidth used | roofline IQ3_S | roofline Coder | roofline Q2_0 |
|:--|---:|---:|---:|
| 1.6 TB/s (paper) | 310 tok/s | 314 tok/s | 397 tok/s |
| 1.336 TB/s (measured read) | 259 tok/s | 262 tok/s | 332 tok/s |
| dataset 6 B/3.38 bpw (2.54 GB) | 631 tok/s | — | — |

Measured η_CDNA = decode / roofline:

| ctx | IQ3_S decode | η @ 1.6 TB/s | η @ 1.336 TB/s | Q2_0 η @ 1.336 TB/s |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 4 096 | 38.58 | **0.124** | **0.149** | 0.121 |
| 16 384 | 37.75 | 0.122 | 0.146 | 0.118 |
| 65 536 | 34.06 | 0.110 | 0.131 | 0.106 |
| 131 072 | 30.25 | 0.098 | 0.117 | 0.094 |
| 261 888 | 24.39 | 0.079 | 0.094 | 0.076 |

The dataset's **η = 0.18 prior is optimistic but not absurd on the
actual-bytes basis** (0.18 vs 0.15 at 4 k); on the 2.54 GB assumption it would
be 0.06, because that assumption undercounts the trunk. Either way the MI210
is at **12–15 % of its own roofline** at short context and **8–9 %** at 262 k.
The card is not short of HBM bandwidth; the kernels are not issuing enough
work per byte.

The comparison rigs, measured with Strata, land much closer to their own
rooflines (same 5.158 GB/token, single-card HBM):

| rig | HBM | roofline | decode 4 k | η | decode 262 k | η |
|:--|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| L40S 1× | 864 GB/s | 168 | 154.0 | **0.92** | — (does not fit) | — |
| V100 4× | 900 GB/s | 174 | 96.3 | **0.55** | 72.5 | **0.42** |
| Titan RTX 2× | 672 GB/s | 130 | ~87 | **0.67** | ~68 (236 k) | **0.52** |
| **MI210 2× (this)** | 1 600 GB/s | 310 | **38.6** | **0.12** | **24.4** | **0.08** |

The L40/V100/Titan rows are the Strata engine; the MI210 row is llama.cpp
b11785 HIP. The engine difference is real and is not hidden: the MI210's
hardware roofline is 1.8–2.4× higher than the CUDA rigs', yet its measured
decode is 2.2–4× lower. That gap is software, not silicon.

Price assumptions: **€0.20/kWh, energy only**. Capex is **not** amortised:
no purchase price for this node was available, and at 24–39 tok/s the
capital cost per token would dominate. The table is not a total cost of
ownership; it is what the wall meter says.

2 007-token cell is €4.83/Mtok and 0.01151 tok/J: **slightly better energy
per token, 1.7× lower aggregate throughput** (4.7 vs 8.3 tok/s). At equal
short answers the MI210 loses on €/Mtok because its prefill is 5× slower and
the fixed prefill energy is larger. L40 power was not sampled in this
campaign, so no L40 €/Mtok row is claimed; its decode is 4× faster, so at
equal power it would be ~4× better on energy per token.

## 9. MI210 vs the CUDA rigs

| rig | engine | decode 4 k | decode 64 k | decode 262 k | TTFT 4 k | TTFT 262 k |
|:--|:--|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **MI210 2×** | llama.cpp b11785 HIP | **38.6** | **34.1** | **24.4** | **3.05 s** | **344.5 s** |
| L40S 1× | Strata | 154.0 | 140.7 | — (48 GB) | 1.40 s | — |
| V100 4× | Strata | 96.3 | 82.3 | 72.5 | 3.15 s | 70.4 s |
| Titan RTX 2× | Strata | ~87 | ~73 | 68 (236 k) | — | — |

- **Decode**: MI210 is 4.0× slower than L40 at 4 k, 2.5× slower than
  V100-4×, and 2.2× slower than Titan-2×. At 262 k it is 3.0× slower than
  V100-4× and 2.8× slower than Titan-2×.
- **Prefill/TTFT**: the gap is worse. 344.5 s at 262 k vs 70.4 s on V100-4×
  (4.9×) and ~30 s on L40 at 114 k (the L40 cannot serve 262 k).
- **€/Mtok**: energy-only decode is €0.44 at 16 k / €0.93 at 262 k. At 16 k
  that is better than V100-4×'s 262 k decode-only €0.65; at 262 k it is worse
  (€0.93 vs €0.65). It is far behind L40 (4× faster at similar or lower
  power). The 262 k cell cost is dominated by prefill.
- **Verdict**: **do not replace a CUDA rig with this MI210 + llama.cpp
  configuration for decode or €/tok/s.** The MI210's HBM is not the problem;
  a Strata-class HIP engine would need to reach η≈0.5–0.9 to compete, and
  that engine is not available for gfx90a here (see §10).

## 10. Strata on gfx90a (optional attempt)

Strata's HIP backend is documented for wave32 RDNA (gfx11/gfx12); MI210 is
wave64 CDNA2. The attempt is recorded verbatim in
`llm/bench/experiments/amd-mi210/strata-hip.sbatch` and
`strata-runs/2026-10-27-amd-mi210/strata-hip/` (job 38247456).

The configure step refused the target before compiling anything:

```text
CMake Error at cmake/hip_backend.cmake:31 (message):
  Strata HIP supports wave32 gfx1100, gfx1101, gfx1200 and gfx1201
  (unvalidated:
  gfx1010;gfx1011;gfx1012;gfx1102;gfx1103;gfx1030;gfx1031;gfx1032;gfx1034;gfx1150;gfx1151;gfx1152);
  CMAKE_HIP_ARCHITECTURES is 'gfx90a'
Call Stack (most recent call first):
  CMakeLists.txt:116 (include)

-- Configuring incomplete, errors occurred!
```

**Result: no Strata HIP binary for gfx90a.** This is a clean, explicit refusal
(`configure_rc=1`), not a crash or a silent fallback. Strata's only wave64
route is the `STRATA_HIP_GFX906` compatibility build for MI50/gfx906, which is
a different card and was not attempted. The MI210 measurement is therefore a
llama.cpp-only point; there is no Strata-vs-llama.cpp engine comparison on
this hardware.

## 11. MI50 and MI100 — unvalidated estimates, not measurements

Neither card is in this cluster. MI50 is gfx906, which ROCm ≥7 no longer
supports; MI100 is gfx908 (CDNA1). Their rows below are **paper/spec
projections** using the MI210's measured η_CDNA, not measurements, and the
capacity column shows why they are not drop-in MI210 substitutes.

| GPU | arch | VRAM | paper HBM | paper FP16 | IQ3_S residency | projected decode (est.) | status |
|:--|:--|---:|---:|---:|:--|---:|:--|
| MI50 | gfx906 | 16 GB | 1.0 TB/s | 26.5 TFLOPS | no: 84 GB needs ≥6 cards or expert streaming | roofline 194 tok/s × η 0.12–0.15 = **23–29 tok/s** per resident card; layer split/PCIe makes it worse | **unvalidated** |
| MI100 | gfx908 | 32 GB | 1.23 TB/s | 184.6 TFLOPS (matrix) | 2 cards = 64 GB, **still short of 84 GB**; 4 cards = 128 GB | roofline 238 tok/s × η 0.12–0.15 = **29–36 tok/s** for a 2-card layer split | **unvalidated** |
| **MI210** | gfx90a | 64 GB | 1.6 TB/s | 181 TFLOPS | **2 cards = 128 GB, fully resident** | **38.6 measured at 4 k** | **measured** |

Do not import the MI50/MI100 rows into the dataset as measurements. The
projection assumes the same llama.cpp HIP stack and the same η as the MI210;
it says nothing about whether a working gfx906/gfx908 build exists, and the
MI50/MI100 capacity means they would stream experts, which the roofline does
not model. The measured AMD point is MI210 only.

## 12. What failed and why

1. **`math` partition**: `AllowAccounts=math`; the ffa job pends with
   `PartitionConfig`. Only `ffa-preempt` runs it. `--test-only` is not an
   authorization check.
2. **`GGML_HIP_ROCWMMA_FATTN`**: absent from b11785; not passed. The build
   uses FA with the default quant list plus `GGML_CUDA_FA_ALL_QUANTS`.
3. **Row split**: `-sm row` fails at load with
   `device ROCm0 does not support split buffers`. Only layer split works for
   this model/buffer set.
4. **Hybrid KV state restore**: llama-bench's repeated test path logs
   `state_seq_set_data: error loading state: failed to restore kv cache`, and
   one `-r 2` run at an exact 4096 depth hit
   `Memory access fault by GPU node-2 … Reason: Unknown`. A later single-rep
   run at the same 4096 depth completed normally. The deterministic part is
   the state-restore error; all cells are single-rep and use `ctx−1` to avoid
   an exact indexer block boundary. The fault wrote three `gpucore.*` dumps
   (53 GB each) into the submit directory; they were deleted and are not
   results. Watch Lustre quota after any HIP fault.
5. **q8_0 KV**: works, saves ~3 GB, but is 22 % slower at 64 k and 44 % slower
   at 262 k. Not used for the headline cells.
6. **C2 collapse**: `-np 2` at 16 k gives one 8.95 tok/s slot and one
   30.2 tok/s slot (aggregate 8.97, below C1). This is a real serving
   limitation on this backend, not a capacity limit.
7. **PLE-to-GPU**: `-ot per_layer_token_embd=ROCm0` works and is 2 % slower;
   the lazy host-mapped table is the better default.
8. **FA off**: same decode (38.59 vs 38.57 at 4 k), plus a state-restore
   error; not a lever.
9. **No xGMI**: `rocm-smi --showtopo` reports PCIE, 2 hops, weight 40. The
   PCIe MI210 SKU has no peer fabric; the layer-split hand-off is PCIe.
10. **Strata HIP**: see §10.

## 13. Caveats

- **Engine mismatch**: the MI210 is llama.cpp b11785 HIP; the CUDA rows are
  Strata. The comparison answers "what does this AMD node deliver with the
  portable HIP stack", not "what could MI210 do with a Strata-class engine".
- **Single stream, single run** for most cells; the server C1 cells have 2
  repeats at ≤64 k and 1 at ≥131 k. No stability run.
- **Power is GPU package power**, not wall; the €/Mtok is energy-only and
  excludes capex. €0.20/kWh is an assumption.
- **ECC is on** (`sramecc+`); the HBM baseline is 1.336 TB/s, not the 1.6 TB/s
  paper peak. Both rooflines are reported.
- **The 4096 state-restore fault is transient**; it is recorded but not
  reproduced. Do not read it as a deterministic model bug at 4096.
- **Coder/Q2_0 power** was not sampled separately; the power cells are IQ3_S.
  Their active bytes are 5.10 and 4.03 GB/token, so their tokens/J should be
  slightly better at the same power, not worse.
- **MI50/MI100 are unvalidated estimates**; see §11.
