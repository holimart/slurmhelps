pairs resident (100 %)** at 262k with 3,072 slots per GPU (5.4–6.2 GiB each),
KV streaming 32,768 of 262,144 cells in VRAM plus 0.77 GiB pinned RAM.

### Q2_0 on 2× at 262k

The smaller pack **does fit fully resident on 2× at 262k**: the engine reports
**24,576 of 24,576 expert pairs (100 %)**, 12,288 slots (15.82 GiB) on
CUDA1, 8.6 GiB free on CUDA0 and 7.1 GiB free on CUDA1 with everything

## R2 — NVLink / P2P

### Hardware

`nvidia-smi topo -m` is **NV2 for all six pairs** (bonded NVLinks); each GPU
reports six 25.781 GB/s links and P2P `OK` in `topo -p2p r/w`. The node's
`nvidia-smi nvlink --help` *does* expose throughput counters (`-gt d`, data
payload) in addition to `-s`/`-c`; the counters are used below.

`p2pbench` (256 MiB, 20 iterations, CUDA events, the engine's own
`libcudart.so.12`, `cudaMemcpyPeerAsync` after explicit
`cudaDeviceEnablePeerAccess`):

| test | GB/s |
|:--|---:|
| local D2D (HBM sanity) | 407.5 |
| peer 0→1 (same NUMA pair) | **48.5** |
| peer 0→2 (cross NUMA pair) | **48.5** |
| `cudaMemcpyAsync(Default)` 0→1 | **48.5** |
| D2H (PCIe, one direction) | 13.1 |
| H2D (PCIe, one direction) | 12.4 |
| GPU0 → pinned host → GPU1 | 12.8 |

Peer copies are **3.8× the host route** and identical for the same-NUMA and
cross-NUMA pair, which is what NVLink (not PCIe) looks like. The 48.5 GB/s
plateau is the copy-engine limit of a 256 MiB `cudaMemcpyPeerAsync`, not the
link limit: the hardware is 6 × 25.781 ≈ 150 GB/s per direction, and a
copy-engine copy on V100 is known to sit below that. The **engine's 12.4 GB/s
figure is exactly the H2D probe** it prints (`PCIe probe: 12.4 GB/s
host->device`), i.e. a PCIe number used for expert-miss cost, not a
measurement of the stage hand-off.
